Problem Definition

Project title: Quality Improvement for a Community Sports Club Player and Volunteer Management System

Background

Most community sports clubs still run on a mix of spreadsheets, emails and group chats to manage players, volunteers and teams. This works okay when a club is small, but once there are multiple teams, age groups and volunteers involved, things start to break down. Player details end up duplicated across different spreadsheets, team lists get out of date, and volunteers don't always have the information they need in time.

Who is affected

Players and parents deal with registration and expect to be placed in the right team without mix-ups. Volunteers need clear, up to date information about their roles and schedules. Coaches rely on accurate rosters and attendance records for training and matches. Club administrators are the ones stuck trying to keep all of this consistent, and end up spending a lot of time on manual admin work instead of running the club.

The problems we're seeing

- Duplicate player records because data is entered manually in more than one place
- Players ending up on the wrong team due to no shared, validated data source
- Volunteer information going missing or being incomplete
- Reporting being slow because someone has to manually pull numbers together
- Overall admin workload being higher than it should be, mostly because the tools being used weren't built for this

Why this fits a software quality assurance project

This isn't just a "the app is missing a feature" problem. It touches both functional issues (registration, team allocation, attendance, reporting) and non-functional ones (usability, reliability, data accuracy, maintainability). That gives us enough to work with for requirements analysis, building a prototype, writing test cases, tracking defects, and mapping requirements to tests across the semester.

Is it doable in a semester

Yes. We're planning to build a prototype using sample data to represent a small to medium sized club, so we don't need access to any real club's data or systems. That keeps the scope realistic for three people working across one semester.
